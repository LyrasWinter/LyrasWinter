500 Word Entry into NYC Midnight — "Aliens and Poor Souls"

We would look like aliens to these poor souls. Consumed by stark white hazmat suits, it is crucial to avoid any contact with the infected. Sweat glistens on every inch of her body; long blonde hair plasters her forehead, eyes moving rapidly under softly closed lids. A whimper escapes her and I hope the dream isn't too horrific. When we found her, she was already under, one of her cats nibbling her hand, clearly feeling nothing in her unconscious state.

Hoisting the stretcher up into the ambulance, we secure the bed for the journey to St. Paul's Hospital, the only medical institution with a lab researching the virus. Closing the large doors of the vehicle, my wrist twinges. No, stings. Tearing off my gloves, I attack my itchy wrist, a nasty bite mark blooming. A black dot flicks out onto me, stark contrast against the white of the hazmat suit. A flea must have come off the woman's cat, somehow worming into my glove. Brushing it off, I sanitize my hands thoroughly, then refasten the glove.

The ambulance gets moving and we do our best to make her comfortable. Few people emerge from dreaming. The virus attacks your temporal lobe, the hippocampus, rendering the victim unconscious, stuck in a state of REM sleep. Those that do wake up report the dreams were never pleasant. That it dug into them, ripping out their darkest fears and pulling them to the surface. Descriptions of feeling like they were under for an eternity, existing in a hell. These reports birthed a fitting name for the virus: The Nightmare.

The woman's hand shoots out latching onto my forearm and I shriek. Fingers claw around me in an iron grip, nails boring into the hazmat suit. Thrashing violently on the stretcher, she screeches a sound emulating nails dragging down a chalkboard. Simultaneously, headlights blind us as a car drives down the wrong side of the motorway, headed straight for us. The ambulance brakes slam, veering us left. Clipping the barrier of the motorway, the transportation unit flips. Medical equipment suspends in air defying gravity for the briefest moment. We stop rolling, skidding along the road on the van's side. Everything crashes down and I brutally hit my head on the ambulance's window now beneath me with a sickening crack causing me to black out.

A gentle hand touches my face and I pry my eyes open. Through blurred double vision, blonde hair consumes my field of sight. Two images slowly morph as my vision returns. Pinning my wrists, the patient pushes me into the shattered glass, shards biting into now exposed skin. Leaning down, a guttural whisper escapes her lips.

"You didn't save me."

I try to push her off but she has me pinned with unnatural strength. The pressure against the broken surface forces the glass further into my back and I scream. Terror seeping from me, I feel like I'm trapped in a fucking nightmare; I want this to end.
